\## **Chapter 3** # **METHODOLOGY** The present chapter describes the methodology applied to evaluate the location of short-term rentals (STR) in Laguna, Philippines. The research process was quantitative in nature due to the utilization of STR’s performance data, demographics, and spatial information in a geographic information system environment. Initially, data was gathered, combined, and structured into spatial and demographic attributes. Using Random Forest, regression models were developed to forecast STR’s annual revenue and occupancy independently. Then, the produced models were applied to score the spatial attributes of every grid cell to approximate the potential STR performance in the study area. The obtained values were combined with selected spatial attractiveness indicators to formulate a suitability score that was objectified using weights. Finally, scores were classified and visualized in a thematic map. ### **Research Design** The study implements a quantitative, predictive, and spatial-analytical research design. The study uses a quantitative research design since it tries to examine numerical data on short-term-rental (STR) listings, demographics, and geography. The study uses a predictive research design since it seeks to predict continuous short-term-rental outcomes (revenue and occupancy) using the Random Forest regression technique. The study applies a spatial-analytical research design because the predictors and outcomes are associated with specific spatial locations in Laguna. The study is set up in two stages. First, it develops and evaluates Random Forest regression models using listing-level STR observations and location and demographic predictors to predict continuous STR revenue and occupancy. Second, it applies the developed models to the corresponding spatial and demographic characteristics of the grid cells to obtain predicted STR performance potential for the study area. The obtained results are then combined with selected spatial-attractiveness indicators to create a composite suitability score for each grid cell. The study, then, proceeds along the following general lines: ![image-5]\(images/image-5.png) Figure 2. Methodological Framework for Predicting Short-Term Rental Performance and Assessing Spatial Suitability in Laguna The present study does not intend to establish a causal relationship between specific variables and STR performance; instead, predictive models were created in order to determine trends and predict the performance level based on the combination of characteristics. ### **Study area** The study area is a province of the Philippines called Laguna. The province is a good area of research because it has different kinds of municipalities and cities which vary in terms of population distribution, tourism resources, accessibility, land use, and the degree of urbanization. In other words, the territory includes both urban and suburban settlements located near Metro Manila as well as rural, lakeside, agriculture-based, and tourism-based regions. The variability of geographic and socioeconomic characteristics in Laguna presented an appropriate basis for investigating whether distinct spatial environments are associated with differing levels of STR performance. This study conceptualized the province as a spatially heterogeneous area rather than presupposing that factors associated with STR performance are homogenous throughout the province. The extent of our study area is defined by the administrative boundary of Laguna. All spatial input data are restricted to the study area by extent clipping before the analytical grid is created. ### **Data Sources** OpenStreetMap (OSM) supplies geographical data about networks of roads, means of transport, points of interest, services and tourism-related places. This data are used to derive spatial predictors representing the accessibility, POI distribution, and tourism characteristics surrounding each analytical unit Other tourism-related data sets are incorporated when pertinent to the study area to represent tourist attractions and other tourism resources. A set of variables was chosen based on the available data, their relevance to the research objectives, and whether they can be used consistently in the spatial analysis. The principal data sources are summarized below. | Data Source | Main Data Obtained | Role in the Study || --- | --- | --- || AirROI | STR listings, annual revenue, occupancy, geographic coordinates | STR performance targets and listing observations || Philippine Statistics Authority | Population and demographic information | Demographic predictors || OpenStreetMap | Roads, transportation facilities, POIs, amenities, and tourism-related locations | Spatial predictors || Tourism-related sources, where applicable | Tourist attractions and related resources | Tourism-related spatial predictors | Table 1. Overview of Primary Data Sources and Feature Contributions ### **Data Preprocessing** The raw data from various sources need to be preprocessed before their spatial integration and usage in the development of the model. The following procedures are undertaken to ensure that the observations will be in consistent and adequate format for statistical and spatial analysis. For the AirROI database, duplicate listings are removed during the data preparation stage. Furthermore, the listings removed from consideration are those previously determined as dormant ones. Such a decision is based on the fact that these properties’ zero revenue and occupancy values should not be used to train Random Forest regressors because they signify the property status quo rather than the result of its operations. In turn, the valid active listings include those with non-zero revenue and occupancy figures that can be used to train the model. The data sets’ missing values are handled consistently with the data-cleaning rules derived from the initial analysis. The monthly historical performance data is used to identify and, if possible, correct inconsistencies in the listing’s trailing-twelve-month revenue and occupancy data before the final targets are built. The PSA demographic data are examined for consistency in geographic names and recorded where necessary. OpenStreetMap and other spatial datasets are screened for invalid or duplicate entries, and POIs are filtered according to the predefined categories used in the analysis. Spatial data are also assessed for valid coordinates and transformed into a common projected coordinate system. The missing values in the data entries are handled according to the procedures. Numeric and categorical variables are subjected to the appropriate imputations or removed if necessary. The extreme values are screened using the criteria for outliers. The preprocessing stages are applied to ensure that the data used for spatial analysis and model development are consistent and reliable. #### **Spatial Data Integration** Because of the different sources, representations, and the fact that they were obtained at different times, the datasets were combined into one for joint analysis that reflects the common spatial context. First, the latitude and longitude of the AirROI and OSM locations were selected and standardized to ensure that their projections conform to the same coordinate system. Each AirROI listing was then associated with the coordinate grid that contained its coordinates; meanwhile, the data elements that described demographic and spatial characteristics of the areas were related to the corresponding grid cells by spatial join and spatial feature calculation procedures. The integration of data included such operations as joining: ![image-6]\(images/image-6.png) Figure 3. Spatial Data Integration Framework The integrated data provide the listing-level observations needed to develop a Random Forest model, as well as the grid-level spatial and demographic predictors needed to apply the trained models and perform the suitability assessment. Grid Construction A regular spatial grid serves as the main frame of reference for this study. In particular, this grid allows for the integration of data obtained at different scales into the analysis. The grid spans the entire territory of Laguna and has a cell size of 1km x 1km. The latter was selected iteratively for the purposes of this study. This size was chosen for three main reasons. Firstly, Airbnb listing coordinates are deliberately shifted by up to about 150 meters to protect the host privacy. Wang et al. (2023) encountered the same problem with Edinburgh data and resolved it by aggregating the coordinates into zones averaging 2.4 square kilometers. The cell size below one square kilometer risks shifting a significant portion of the listings into the wrong cell. Secondly, the demographic data used in this study are available at the municipal level. A 1 km grid provides a finer spatial framework for integrating these demographic data with the spatial characteristics of individual locations. Thirdly, a 1 kilometer grid would generate 1,928 cells in the province spanning roughly 64 cells per municipality or city on average. This sample size matches the typical size of a previous Random Forest study on short term rentals and real estate (Kirkos, 2022; Iban, 2022) while still having enough variety within each cell not to collapse all the observations into a single value as suggested by Levantesi and Piscopo (2020). The analysis was repeated on a 500 meter and 2 kilometer grids to check if the result holds for different cell sizes since there is not an established correct cell size for this type of study (Renigier Biłozor et al., 2022). Each AirROI listing is associated with the grid cell containing its coordinates. OSM features are associated with the relevant grid cells according to their spatial relationship with each cell. Demographic attributes are assigned to grid cells according to the municipality in which each cell is located. The grid is the shared spatial framework for applying the trained Random Forest models and executing the composite suitability assessment. The listing level observations remain as individual observations for the purposes of Random Forest model training, with grid cells serving as the spatial units for generating predictions and calculating suitability scores. ### **Spatial Feature Engineering** The engineered variables are used as predictors on geographic and demographic locations in the Random Forest models and selected spatial-attractiveness indicators in the suitability assessment based on the utilization in the analytical framework. Spatial feature engineering is performed to transform the input spatial information into numeric features describing characteristics and accessibility of each spatial unit. The engineered features are categorized into several groups. Accessibility Features Accessibility features can be defined as the connection between a grid cell and the relevant transportation and tourism-related locations to which it may have an impact on STR demand. The distance measures are determined by the coordinates of the relevant locations. The following POI categories are considered: restaurants, commercial, transportation, recreation, tourist attraction, and other facilities. POI density is defined by the number of POIs of different categories found within the search areas around the identified candidate points. This indicator characterizes the degree of provision of services and opportunities around the future STR location. #### **Tourism Features** Tourism features represent presence, concentration, or accessibility of tourism related resources within or near each grid cell. Attractions, distance-to-attraction, and other tourism resource metrics included in the dataset are examples of tourism features. Final tourism variables are determined by availability and spatial accuracy of the tourism data. #### **Demographic Features** The demographic information obtained from the PSA were linked to their corresponding spatial grid cell based on municipality. Population density and other demographic information were converted to numeric predictors. Subsequently, the attributes were included in the Random Forest models to evaluate if demographic information surrounding a certain area can predict STR revenue and occupancy. ### **Random Forest Regression** Random Forest regression is used to predict two short-term rental performance measures: annual revenue and occupancy. Two separate predictive models are developed: #### **Model 1: STR Revenue Prediction** The first Random Forest regression model estimates annual STR revenue based on the selected spatial, demographic, and tourism-related predictors. #### **Model 2: STR Occupancy Prediction** The second Random Forest regression model estimates STR occupancy based on the selected spatial, demographic, and tourism-related predictors. The geographic location of each listing is associated with the corresponding spatial and demographic predictors, with the data processed using Random Forest Regression (algorithm from the scikit-learn Python library). Random Forests are ensembles of decision trees, constructed on bootstrap samples of the original data. Results from these trees are then combined, specifically, the continuous variable is estimated by simple averaging of results. In particular, this algorithm was chosen because there might be non-linear relations between the dependent variable and explanatory factors, and between different independent variables. Therefore, using a non-linear model, such as Random Forest Regression, could provide better results. The data was split into training and test set with ratio 80:20. The test set was used to evaluate the final prediction results, whereas the training set was used for training the model. Specifically, this study conducted 5-fold cross-validation with grid search to select the best parameters based on the training data, which were further used for the final model and evaluated by test set. The hyperparameters including number of trees, max depth, min samples split, min samples leaf, and max features were considered in this study. The final model was evaluated using RMSE, MAE, and R² to assess its predictive performance. Grid-Level Prediction Once the random forest revenue and occupancy models are trained and validated, the resulting trained models are applied to the spatial and demographic predictors characterizing the grid cells encompassing Laguna. Since the revenue and occupancy models use attributes related to location and demographics as predictors rather than specific property characteristics, predicting the performance of a given STR listing is possible irrespective of the presence of an active STR listing. Thus, the trained revenue model predicts an annual revenue value for every grid cell, while the trained occupancy model predicts an occupancy value for every grid cell. These predicted values represent the general STR performance potential associated with the characteristics of a given location and are used as performance indicators in the composite suitability assessment. ### **Model Evaluation** The predictive performance of the Random Forest regression models is evaluated using Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), and the coefficient of determination (R²). #### **Mean Absolute Error** Mean Absolute Error measures the average absolute difference between observed and predicted values: ![image-10]\(images/image-10.png) Equation 2. The feature-importance procedure employed in the study comprises permutation importance since it assesses relevance based on the drop in predictive performance on held-out data, as opposed to training-data splits, rendering it more reliable in cases where predictors are of highly varying nature and scale, which is the case with the spatial, demographic, POI, and listing attributes used in this study (Iban, 2022). This approach also conforms to the study’s predictive, as opposed to causal, framing of feature importance, since the importance score directly reflects the contribution to prediction accuracy, as opposed to a presumed causal effect (Iban, 2022). For each Random Forest model, the set of importance values relating to the predictors is retrieved and evaluated. Feature-importance outcomes are obtained separately for revenue and occupancy models. The analysis emphasizes the relevance of demographic, spatial, accessibility, tourism, and POI attributes. The results are utilized to identify the characteristics that contain the largest amount of predictive information about STR performance. The notion of feature importance is understood in terms of predictive power, rather than causal impact. Hence, a variable that obtains a high value of importance should be regarded as having a significant predictive role in the model, but its variation is not necessarily producing changes in STR revenue and occupancy. ### **Composite Suitability Scoring** After the predictive models are evaluated, their predictions are combined in a composite spatial suitability assessment. The composite score is designed to reflect multiple STR investment suitability dimensions rather than focusing on one performance measure. Suitability analysis integrates predicted STR performance along with selected spatial-attractiveness indicators. The Jenks Natural Breaks classification method was used to classify the continuous suitability values into five classes by dividing the grid cells based on the natural grouping of composite values. The resulting categories are: - Very High Suitability- High Suitability- Moderate Suitability- Low Suitability- Very Low Suitability Classification is applied uniformly to all cells of the grid. The classes obtained are intended to reflect relative differences in suitability within the study area, not absolute values of pro tability. This classification allows a portrayal of potential for STR investment in a form accessible to an understanding of an individual municipality, or of other administrative divisions, without assigning a numeric rank to any. ### **Spatial Visualization** The predictive and suitability outputs are represented geographically using GIS-based visualization. The following spatial outputs are generated: - Spatial distribution of observed STR listings;- Spatial distribution of predicted revenue;- Spatial distribution of predicted occupancy;- Spatial distribution of important spatial and demographic predictors;- Spatial distribution of the composite suitability score; and- Suitability-classification map. The final suitability map shows the categorization of individual grid cells across Laguna. It enables the recognition of grid cells that possess varying levels of suitability. In the case of a web-based decision-support interface, the processed results can be retrieved through well-defined data endpoints and be visualized using an interactive geographic interface. The interface serves as a mere presentation layer for the obtained results and does not affect any of the underlying calculations. The predicted revenue and occupancy maps represent location-level performance potential derived from the trained Random Forest models. ### **Model and Data Validation** Validation is performed at various points in the analysis pipeline. First, the processed datasets are inspected for missing, duplicate, invalid, or inconsistent observations prior to modeling. Second, the Random Forest models are validated using cross-validation during model development and then tested on the held-out test dataset. Mean absolute error (MAE), root mean squared error (RMSE), and R 2 score are used as metrics to assess predictive performance. Third, we validate the spatial outputs to make sure that the grid cells have the correct spatial joins, distances, POIs, and attributes. The grid-level prediction stage is also validated by confirming that all required spatial and demographic predictors are available for each grid cell and that the trained revenue and occupancy models produce predictions for the complete spatial grid. Finally the composite suitability calculations are validated by ensuring that the process of indicator normalization, calculation of entropy weights, and final suitability scores are in agreement and that the obtained weights add up to 1.
